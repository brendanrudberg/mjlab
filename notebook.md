ARMATURE = 1.5e-4 * 19.61^2 = 0.0577 kg*m^2

This is a LOWER BOUND. It excludes the gearbox's own rotating mass, which the

**Gains, from BeyondMimic (arXiv 2508.08241) Supplementary S1.** Sergio pointed to
this as the design philosophy behind the G1 constants.
- k_p = I * w_n^2, k_d = 2 * zeta * I * w_n, where I is the armature only
- They use armature alone because subtree inertia varies with configuration and
  exact precision isn't required
- zeta = 2 (overdamped) specifically compensates for that underestimate. These two
  choices go together.
- w_n = 10 Hz. Their ablation (Fig S2) tested 5/10/25 Hz; 25 Hz gave slightly
  better local tracking but produced audible gearbox oscillation from torque
  overshoot, so they rejected it. 10 Hz promotes compliance with moderate gains.
- Action scale alpha = 0.25 * tau_max / k_p, assuming contacts occur near the
  default pose and max joint torque scales with expected load.
- Their armature ablation: setting it to zero causes excessive joint accelerations,
  overswinging, and self-collisions. They call it a real physical contribution,
  not a numerical stability knob. Good support for replacing Diya's 0.01 with 0.0577.

Full day on the ME 90 background and objectives report. Most of the time went

**Co-design literature.** Searched it properly for the first time. The field
calls what I'd been describing "co-design" and it goes back further than I
assumed (Sims 1994 for evolutionary morphology search; analytic model-based
methods predate the learning-based work). Read C² (arXiv 2209.06579) in full.

Taxonomy from their related work:
- Analytic vs data-driven. Analytic requires deriving equations of motion and
  hand-specifying constraints per candidate design. Data-driven learns the
  policy through interaction, so no analytical model per design.
- Morphology-changing vs topology-unchanging. Topology-changing methods produce
  structures that are asymmetric or place motors unreasonably, so they aren't
  physically deployable.
- Most data-driven methods are bi-level: inner loop trains a policy for a
  candidate, outer loop picks the next candidate from the resulting fitness.
  Expensive, and most of the field's effort targets that cost.

C² specifically: a population network conditioned on morphology parameters acts
as a surrogate Q-function, so you can score a design without training a policy
for it. Their contribution is fixing two failures in that scheme (exploration
error from offline training, and distribution shift when warm-starting the
individual network on a new morphology).

**Correction I had to make.** I'd drafted an opening claiming this project
"inverts" the usual order by using RL to drive hardware design. That's roughly
what the entire co-design field does, and EA-CoRL's intro says nearly the same
sentence. GLAMDRING (arXiv 2609.19452) is closest to my actual method: they
resolve link lengths and actuators post-hoc from the policy's logged operating
envelope. So the logged-envelope idea isn't mine to claim.

What is distinct: I'm not searching the design space at all. One proposed
design, one policy, read off the loads. That's one iteration of a
human-in-the-loop design cycle, not automated co-design.

**Second correction, bigger.** I'd built the whole framing around co-design,

**PPO internals.** Worked through the loop properly rather than treating it as
a black box: collect with a frozen policy (24 steps x 4096 envs), compute GAE
advantages and detach them, then ~5 epochs of updates on that batch, discard,
repeat. The probability ratio exists for importance sampling (the data came
from the pre-update policy); the clip is what makes reusing the batch safe.
Critic exists to give per-step credit, which is what makes this tractable on
500-step episodes.

**Planar base support implemented.** Built in the order classification ->

Classification:
- `RootType` enum (FIXED / FLOATING / PLANAR). `is_fixed_base` now derived from it,
  so its nine existing call sites mean "welded to the world" again.
- `get_planar_joints` in `utils/spec.py`: matches the root body's joints against
  slide-x, slide-z, hinge-y by type and normalized axis, returns them ordered
  (x, z, pitch). Falls through to FIXED instead of raising, so fixed-base arms
  hinged to the world keep their articulation.
- First version classified the C1 as FIXED anyway. Cause: mjlab wraps every
  entity without a freejoint in a `mocap_base` body at load, before classification
  runs. `root_body` was the frozen wrapper, so every pose read was also pointing at
  a body that never moves. Fix: planar pass-through in `auto_wrap_fixed_base_mocap`.

Plumbing bugs hit along the way, all single-cause mass failures:
- Removed the `is_fixed_base` field from `EntityData` -> ~400 TypeErrors.
- A leftover `is_fixed_base` property in `EntityData` became the dataclass field's
  default -> "non-default argument follows default argument."
- `RootType` defined in both `entity.py` and `data.py`. Two enums with the same
  members never compare equal, so every floating-base write silently did nothing
  (17 failures). Kept one definition in `data.py`, re-exported from the package.

Then:
- Keyframe writer prepends (x, z, pitch) in the XML's declaration order. The old
  writer put the leg angles into the base slots.
- Indexing: `planar_joint_q_adr` / `v_adr`, base joints excluded from `joint_q_adr`.
  This also fixed the observation leak: base joints had been counted as
  articulation, so the policy would have observed its own world x position.
- Pose writes extract pitch as `2*atan2(qy, qw)`, wrapped to (-pi, pi]. Not
  `euler_xyz_from_quat`: its arcsine caps pitch at ±90°, which folds any flip.
- Two velocity helpers: root orientation from qpos for either type, and the
  world-frame qvel write. Planar needs no rotation since the base joints hang off
  the world.
- `velocity_command.py:131` reads orientation through the now-public helper.
